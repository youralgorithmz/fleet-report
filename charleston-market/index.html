The short version

  • The homes are worth the call. Median owner-occupied value is $489,100 on the Census measure and $618,000 on Zillow's, against $259,000 for South Carolina. Median household income is $88,494, 28% above the state. A $500 minimum job is not a stretch here; it is a small pruning ticket.
  • The canopy is mature. 101,311 of the county's 209,803 housing units were built before 1990, and 121,738 are detached single-family homes. Peninsula ZIPs have a median build year of 1938 to 1964. Old neighbourhoods mean big trees close to roofs.
  • Storms keep the phones ringing. The National Weather Service logged 866 storm events in the county from 2015 to 2025; 299 of the reports describe downed trees or limbs. Ten named tropical systems crossed it in eight years, Matthew and Irma through Ian and Idalia.
  • The competition is thin online. Of 92 tree companies we located in the county, 29 publish no website at all and another 11 have sites that fail to load, show an expired certificate or sit on a parked domain. Only 37 publish an email address. Four national brands are present; the rest are owner-operators.
  • The territory is open. We run one contractor per ZIP at a time and nobody holds Charleston County with us today. Whoever signs first gets the county's ZIPs exclusively for as long as they run.

Every figure on this page comes from a public record or from our own operating rules, and the source for each is listed at the bottom. Nothing here is a projection of what you would earn.

What the county looks like

Charleston County had 408,235 residents at the 2020 census and an estimated 436,200 by 2025, growth of about 7% in five years. Nearly a fifth of the Census 2020–24 housing stock is on the peninsula and in West Ashley and James Island, where lots are older and trees are largest. Mount Pleasant and Johns Island carry the newest and most expensive detached housing.

Home values have doubled in ten years, and pulled away from every neighbour

Zillow's typical home value for Charleston County was $311,110 in July 2016 and $617,997 in July 2026. The neighbouring counties and the other Southeastern markets we watch rose too, but from a lower base and by less. A homeowner with a $600,000 house protects the roof, the driveway and the insurance premium; that is who answers a text about a leaning oak.

Eleven years of storm reports, and trees in a third of them

We pulled every National Weather Service storm event logged for Charleston County from January 2015 to the end of the 2025 file. There were 866. Reading the narratives, 299 describe trees or limbs down, and 54 describe a tree landing on a house, vehicle, fence or other structure. Thunderstorm wind is the most common event at 264, coastal flooding second at 210. Ten named tropical systems appear in the record.

What one appointment is

A homeowner in your ZIPs who replied, described work with at least $500 of potential, gave an address we could place, and took a time on your calendar that a human verified.

What it costs

$1,500 per tier per week, due Monday. A tier is one protected county-based territory with the data, texting, qualification, booking and verification bundled. No per-lead fees.

What we have seen

Across our current contractors, about 20 quote appointments per contractor per week is the observed average and roughly 95% of booked homeowners are there when the estimator arrives, by our owners' count. Both are averages, not commitments.

What the yield looks like

Our operating average is one booked, verified, qualified appointment for every 1,250 texts sent. Pacing decisions during the week are made on that number.

What we promise, and what we refuse to

The contractors who stay with us are the ones who heard this part first.

We promise

  • An opportunity to bid: a willing homeowner, a real address in your territory, a time on your calendar, checked by a person.
  • Your ZIPs are yours alone while you run with us. We never text the same ZIP for two contractors at the same time.
  • Nothing is sent in your name without a named owner of ours approving it first.
  • Every appointment carries its evidence: the conversation, the qualification and the verification record.

We do not promise

  • A job, a close rate, or revenue. The bid is yours to win.
  • A fixed number of appointments. Twenty a week is what we have seen on average, not what we guarantee.
  • That a homeowner will always show. About 95% do by our count; some will not.
  • A hold on your ZIPs after you stop. Exclusivity lasts exactly as long as you run.

What this brief did not cover

We did not test where any individual company ranks in local search, count reviews, price the market, or examine the City of Charleston and Mount Pleasant tree-protection ordinances and permitting, all of which matter to a bid. We did not survey homeowners. The competitor count is companies we could find from public sources on one day; there are certainly crews we missed. Census ZIP figures are five-year estimates with margins of error that widen in small ZIPs. Storm counts depend on what the Weather Service logged and how its narratives were written.
