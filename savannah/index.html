Territory yield analysis · August 2026

The Savannah Pattern

Tree Masters of Savannah books more appointments per person texted than almost any territory in the fleet, and it does it every week no matter how many times the area is re-run. This pulls the live appointment records for all 30 subaccounts, tests whether that reputation survives contact with the data, works out what Chatham County actually has that the weaker territories don't — and screens every county in the United States for the same profile.

The claim, tested

Half right — and the half that's wrong is worth more money

The premise was that Savannah performs, week after week, however often we re-run the area. Three separate claims sit inside that. The records answer each one differently.

✓

It books at the top of the fleet

104 appointments in August, second-highest of 30 subaccounts — from the second-smallest contact list of the fourteen subaccounts that booked 70 or more. Per 10,000 people texted it books 14.1, against a fleet median of 8.1. That is 1.7× the typical territory.

✓

It really does turn up every week

14, 41, 22, 27 booked across the four August weeks. No dead week, and only seven subaccounts held a higher floor. Eight had at least one week with nothing at all.

!

But it loses a third of what it books — exactly like everyone else

34 of 104 cancelled, a 32.7% cancellation rate against a fleet median of 32.9%. Savannah is elite at getting the appointment and completely average at keeping it. Fix that one number and it is the best territory in the company by a distance.

Consistency

Every week, from a list that keeps getting re-run

73,735 contacts across ten Chatham County ZIP codes — a small territory worked repeatedly. If re-running an area exhausted it, the weekly line would decay. It doesn't.

Where it ranks

Yield, not volume, is the honest comparison

Raw appointment counts reward whoever holds the biggest list. Dividing by contacts asks the real question: how much does this territory give back per person we text? Switch the measure to see how differently the fleet sorts.

The AI is the better booker here too. 81% of Savannah's appointments (84 of 104) came from the conversational assistant, and those cancelled at 33%; the 20 booked by other paths cancelled at 30%. Fleet-wide the assistant booked 1,374 with a 26% cancellation rate against 40% for every other path. Switching it off would make the numbers worse, not better.

What the area has

Chatham County is a storm-struck oak forest with houses in it

Six county-level measures, each indexed against the fleet median territory. Savannah is not special because it is rich — homes there are worth less than the fleet median. It is special because trees keep falling on things, and there are comparatively few companies to call.

The canopy is old, big, and failing

Chatham County is about 36% tree canopy, down from 50% in 1992, dominated by live oak. Savannah replanted with live oaks around 120 years ago after the 1893 hurricane, and roughly 15,000 of them are now reaching the end of their lifespan — the city is already replacing them as they fail. An aging population of very large trees standing over houses is a standing order book for removals and structural pruning, independent of any storm.

And the weather keeps knocking them over

347 damaging-wind events in eleven years, including 47 tropical events — 7.6× the fleet median territory. Matthew, Irma, Dorian, Ian, Idalia, Debby and Helene all reached Chatham. In Helene alone the county reported roughly 240 blocked road sections, 177 downed power lines, and 66 felled trees inside city limits.

What actually predicts a good territory

The fleet is a 29-market natural experiment. It disagrees with the obvious answer.

Same script, same assistant, same offer, 29 different American markets. Correlating each territory's yield against its county's characteristics separates what matters from what only sounds like it should.

Home value works backwards. The single strongest relationship in the fleet is that cheaper markets book better (r = −0.62), not richer ones. Be careful with it: expensive markets in this fleet are also the big saturated metros with the most competing tree companies, so price, market size and competition are tangled together and one month of data cannot pull them apart. It is a reason to stop assuming affluent suburbs are the prize — not a reason to go chase the poorest counties in America, where a $500 minimum job stops being realistic.

Where else looks like this

Thirteen unassigned counties with Savannah's shape

Every US county was scored on distance from Chatham County across seven measures, then filtered to what the fleet has actually proven it can work: home values between $200k and $460k, population 80k–650k, under 4.5 tree firms per 10,000 residents, and real tree-on-property damage history. Counties already assigned to a contractor were removed.

The three to look at first

Dorchester and Berkeley Counties, SC — the Summerville and Goose Creek side of Charleston. Same coastal live-oak belt, same hurricane corridor (Idalia 2023, Debby 2024), home values within $40k of Savannah's, and 2.3–3.1 tree firms per 10,000 residents. Charleston County itself fails the screen on price, at $618k. Leon County, FL — Tallahassee runs about 55% urban tree canopy, close to the highest of any city its size in the country, on top of 47 damaging-wind events a year and the 2024 tornadoes.

One that scores well and shouldn't be trusted yet

Bay County, FL ranks second, but Hurricane Michael destroyed roughly 80% of its canopy in 2018. The storm history that lifts its score is the same event that removed the mature trees the work depends on; replanting since 2025 is still saplings. The screen measures where trees have fallen, which is not always where trees still stand — verify canopy on the ground before buying that territory.

Two things found on the way

Worth someone's attention before the next launch

!

Six Savannah appointments landed outside the signed ZIP list

The signed marketing list holds ten ZIPs. August appointments also appeared in 31415, 31408, 31407, 31303 and 31324, while 31401 and 31302 produced none. Separately, territory-map.csv records 31401, 31404, 31407, 31408 and 31415 against a client labelled "Savannah", which may or may not be this same contractor. That is a records question under the territory-integrity rule, not a proven breach — but it needs resolving before a neighbouring tier is sold.

!

21% of Savannah's appointments carry no address

22 of 104. Fleet-wide the figure is 366 of 1,889. An appointment with no address cannot be routed, cannot be checked against territory, and cannot be counted in any geographic analysis — including this one.

What this cannot tell you

The honest edges

  • One month
    The calendar API returns appointment history from 3 August 2026 onward for these locations. "Week after week" is tested over four weeks, not a year. Everything here should be re-run in a month, when there is twice the data.
  • n = 29
    Twenty-nine territories is a small sample for correlation. The relationships shown are strong enough to steer a decision about where to look next; none is strong enough to be called a cause.
  • Tangled
    Home value, market size and competitor density move together across these 29 markets. The data cannot separate their individual contributions.
  • Proxy
    There is no national county-level tree canopy dataset in the public feeds used here. Storm narratives describing fallen trees are a stand-in, and they under-count places with big trees and calm weather while over-counting places whose trees have already been destroyed.
  • Not measured
    Attendance. Across all 1,889 August appointments the only statuses in use are confirmed and cancelled — zero showed, zero no-show, on any subaccount. Nothing here says whether a surviving appointment was actually kept.
  • Not measured
    Messages sent. Yield is per contact in the database, not per text delivered. Two territories with the same contact count may have been messaged at very different intensities.
