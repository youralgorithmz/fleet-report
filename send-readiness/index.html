The 442 shortfall is two causes, neither fixable with the data on hand. Southern Stone, 152: its file only ever held 6,154 usable rows against the 8,045 originally requested, known since Saturday. Alltimate, 290: its Volusia list is now fully consumed and the remainder were genuine opt-outs, which stayed blocked throughout.

The cooldown was overridden, then restored

The first pass delivered 28,100 and held 12,214 more. Those holds were not opt-outs.

The console skips any contact whose CRM conversation was touched in the last 14 days — a rule Nile set on 2026-09-21 after finding that fresh lists booked one appointment per 626–834 texts while the heaviest re-senders ran one per 5,039–10,722. The gbalo files turned out to overlap heavily with recently-contacted people, so the rule caught them: Alltimate alone had 976 contacts texted that same day, 452 texted two days before, 386 the day before.

Joseph overrode it. RESEND_COOLDOWN_DAYS was set to 0, the held contacts were staged as their own lists and sent, and the variable was returned to 14 once the last batch closed — confirmed by reading 14 back from production.

Opt-outs were never part of this

The suppression gate and the cooldown are separate checks. Only the cooldown was disabled; the durable opt-out store stayed enforced on every contact, at enroll time rather than at upload time.

Staging the held contacts on separate lists, rather than re-running the originals, is what kept anyone from getting a second message the same day.

Two faults worth carrying forward

1 · A stalled upload reads as a finished one

Changing a service variable forces a redeploy, and the redeploy killed two list uploads mid-flight — Alltimate at 2,180 of 6,645, Affordable CT at 973 of 4,486. Neither retried. The lists simply sat with a pending count nobody was watching, and the send took only what had landed.

It was caught because Alltimate's numbers did not add up, not because anything reported a problem. POST /api/manage/lists/<id> with {"action":"resume"} restarts one. After any variable change, check for lists left uploading.

2 · The console upload has no territory gate

Alltimate's file mixed 7,109 Edgewater rows (Volusia) with 4,081 Flagler Beach rows (Flagler). Its protocol serves Volusia only and refuses anything outside it with no outbound message — so those 4,081 would have been texted and then ghosted. Contacts enroll in ZIP order and 32136 sorts first, so they would have gone out ahead of every valid one.

The file was re-cut to Edgewater only before any of it sent. scripts/list-import.ts runs this territory check; the console's upload route does not. Purchased lists must be cut by county before they go in.

Cost

The wallet went from $925.25 to $659.85 — $265.40 for 56,477 messages, $0.0047 each. An early estimate of $451 being insufficient was wrong; it assumed roughly twice the real per-message rate.

How these numbers were read. Per-account figures come from the console's own job ledger at /api/manage/send on production, summed across all 24 batches. The cooldown value was read back from /api/manage/overview after the final redeploy. Cost is the Sendillo wallet balance before and after. Nothing here is projected.
