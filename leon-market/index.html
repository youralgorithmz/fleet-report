The short version

  • Trees come down here more than anywhere else we track. Of 639 National Weather Service storm events logged for Leon County from 2015 to 2025, 458 describe trees or limbs down and 111 describe a tree landing on a house, vehicle, fence or power line. That is 15 tree-down reports per 10,000 residents, double Charleston or Savannah. Sixteen tornadoes, ten named tropical systems, and about 44 wind events a year.
  • The canopy is old and the houses sit in it. 72,139 of the county's 137,793 housing units were built before 1990. The city adopted an Urban Forest Master Plan in 2018 to manage a canopy that reaches over almost every neighbourhood north of the interstate.
  • The homes are worth the call in the right ZIPs. Median owner-occupied value is $301,800 countywide, but Killearn, Bradfordville, Buck Lake and Southwood run $320,000 to $432,000 with incomes of $81,000 to $129,000. Our $500 minimum job is a routine ticket there. We would not text the student ZIP around the universities, where only 13% of households own.
  • The competition is thin online. Of 105 tree companies we located in the county, 57 publish no website we could find and another 11 have sites that fail to load or sit on a parked domain. Only 36 publish an email address. 1 national or franchise brand is present; the rest are owner-operators.
  • The territory is open. Leon County ranked sixth in the national screen we ran in August for counties shaped like our best-performing market. We run one contractor per ZIP at a time and nobody holds Leon County with us today. Whoever signs first gets the county's ZIPs exclusively for as long as they run.

Every figure on this page comes from a public record or from our own operating rules, and the source for each is listed at the bottom. Nothing here is a projection of what you would earn.

What the county looks like

Leon County had 292,198 residents at the 2020 census and an estimated 299,048 by 2025. Tallahassee is the only incorporated city and holds two thirds of the population; Bradfordville, Woodville, Chaires, Capitola, Fort Braden and Miccosukee are the unincorporated communities around it. The state government and two universities make the workforce unusually steady for a Florida market, and the owner-occupied homes cluster in the northeast quadrant.

Home values are up two thirds in ten years and have held through the 2025 cooling

Zillow's typical home value for Leon County was $177,873 in July 2016 and $296,441 in July 2026. Jacksonville and Gainesville rose faster and have fallen back further since 2024; Tallahassee, with its government payroll, has flattened rather than dropped. A homeowner with a $300,000 house and a 70-foot pine over the roof protects the roof first.

Eleven years of storm reports, and trees in seven of every ten

We pulled every National Weather Service storm event logged for Leon County from January 2015 to the end of the 2025 file. There were 639, and 489 of them were thunderstorm or high wind. Reading the narratives, 458 describe trees or limbs down and 111 describe a tree landing on a structure, vehicle or power line. The 10 May 2024 outbreak put two EF2 tornadoes through the county, each carrying a $92 million property-damage estimate in the NOAA record; Hurricane Michael in 2018 and Helene in 2024 both passed within reach.

What one appointment is

A homeowner in your ZIPs who replied, described work with at least $500 of potential, gave an address we could place, and took a time on your calendar that a human verified.

What it costs

$1,500 per tier per week, due Monday. A tier is one protected county-based territory with the data, texting, qualification, booking and verification bundled. No per-lead fees.

What we have seen

Across our current contractors, about 20 quote appointments per contractor per week is the observed average and roughly 95% of booked homeowners are there when the estimator arrives, by our owners' count. Both are averages, not commitments.

What the yield looks like

Our operating average is one booked, verified, qualified appointment for every 1,250 texts sent. Pacing decisions during the week are made on that number.

What we promise, and what we refuse to

The contractors who stay with us are the ones who heard this part first.

We promise

  • An opportunity to bid: a willing homeowner, a real address in your territory, a time on your calendar, checked by a person.
  • Your ZIPs are yours alone while you run with us. We never text the same ZIP for two contractors at the same time.
  • Nothing is sent in your name without a named owner of ours approving it first.
  • Every appointment carries its evidence: the conversation, the qualification and the verification record.

We do not promise

  • A job, a close rate, or revenue. The bid is yours to win.
  • A fixed number of appointments. Twenty a week is what we have seen on average, not what we guarantee.
  • That a homeowner will always show. About 95% do by our count; some will not.
  • A hold on your ZIPs after you stop. Exclusivity lasts exactly as long as you run.

What this brief did not cover

We did not test where any individual company ranks in local search, count reviews, price the market, or examine the City of Tallahassee and Leon County tree ordinances and permitting, all of which matter to a bid. We did not survey homeowners. The competitor count is companies we could find from public sources on one day; the Census count of 128 landscaping establishments says there are crews we missed. Census ZIP figures are five-year estimates with margins of error that widen in small ZIPs. Storm counts depend on what the Weather Service logged and how its narratives were written.
