Two rounds, one week

Sequence matters

The event was not one storm. An overnight line crossed the Thumb and Metro Detroit, then a second, larger round fired across mid-Michigan and Oakland County the following afternoon. The second round is the one that produced most of the tree damage — and it finished only hours ago, which is why the list below is fresh and the phones are not yet saturated.

Damage concentration

Grouped by county, because county is the unit territory is assigned in. The bar is the summed damage index across each area’s thirty strongest ZIPs — concentration, not spread. The columns beside it are what turns concentration into a working day.

One number to distrust

Macomb scores 55.1 on damage concentration but holds only two storm reports of its own. Its index is almost entirely borrowed from reports just over the line in Oakland and Wayne. That is a legitimate inference — the storm did not stop at the county line — but it is a weaker evidence base than Oakland’s eleven, all of them tree-specific. Treat Macomb as good SMS inventory, not as a crew destination.

Read this before you read the table

At the default weights the top of this list is Lansing, not Oakland — 48910, 48906, 48911 and 48823 all outrank the corridor. That is not a mistake and it is not being hidden. Those ZIPs took direct hits, hold 11,000–25,000 housing units each, and sit at 140–460 units per km², so they score well on all three terms at once.

The score ranks ZIPs. It does not know that a crew can only be in one place. Oakland wins the deployment on things the score deliberately excludes: 1.55× the damage concentration across its thirty strongest ZIPs (87.8 against 56.8), 1.7× the reachable housing, eleven damage reports every one of which names trees against Lansing’s seven of twelve, the only county-level outage figure in the state at 48,567 customers — and the fact that the crew is already pointed there. Filter this table to Lansing tri-county and you are looking at a genuine second deployment, not a reason to redirect the first.

Where to put the crew

Routing

The crew decision is not the same as the campaign decision, and running them together is the mistake worth avoiding. A campaign can cover 450,000 housing units; a crew can cover whatever sits inside about a twenty-kilometre radius of wherever the truck sleeps.

Ranked on damage alone, the top of the list is the dense inner ring — Hazel Park, Royal Oak, Madison Heights, Warren. Those are excellent SMS targets and mediocre crew targets: small lots, small trees, more renters who cannot authorise the work, and jobs that struggle to clear the $500 qualification floor.

The corridor west and north of Pontiac inverts that. It carries almost the same damage evidence at a fraction of the density — White Lake Township at 119 housing units per km², Highland at 97, Holly at 41, against 1,021 in Hazel Park. Large lots, mature oak and pine, and the heaviest outage load in the state: 22 ZIPs, 155,253 housing units, every one of them with confirmed damage nearby.

Solved independently of the tip: of the 22 corridor ZIPs, 48386 minimises score-weighted travel to all of them. 80% of the corridor’s weight sits within 21.9 km, and the next-best base is only 0.5 km worse.

Second-wave option, if a second crew exists

The Lansing tri-county cluster — Ingham, Clinton, Eaton — took twelve damage reports, seven of them tree-specific, including “over 100 downed trees in the Lake Lansing area”, the largest single concentration reported anywhere in this event. Median density across its ZIPs is 50 housing units per km², the lowest of any area here, which means big properties and big trees. It is 104 km from the Oakland base: a separate deployment, never a detour.

Draft message

Written against §5 of the consent policy: no fabricated urgency, no claim that anyone looked at their property, no promise of a price or an outcome, sender identified, opt-out present.

Hi {{first_name}} — {{setter_name}} with {{company}}, a tree service working in {{township}} after Wednesday night’s storms. We’re booking free estimates in the area this week for anything down, split or hanging. Want someone to take a look? Reply STOP to opt out.

Placeholders resolve per subaccount. “{{township}}” is the Census county subdivision for the recipient’s ZIP — it is in the target list and it is what people actually call where they live.

Timing

  • Quiet hours bind: sends only 7:00 AM–7:00 PM recipient-local. Every ZIP here is Eastern, so the existing Sunday 2:30 PM launch sits inside the window with room for the tail.
  • Storm response decays fast. The second round ended mid-afternoon on 3 September; the useful window for a storm-framed message is days, not weeks.
  • The Storm Prediction Center has the Great Lakes inside its Day 2 area with ongoing thunderstorms expected at 12Z Friday. Re-read the storm reports before launching — a third round would change the ZIP order, not just add to it.
  • DTE reported more than 239,000 customers out at 4:30 PM Thursday and expected 80% restored by end of day. Restoration progress is a fair proxy for when homeowners stop thinking about the utility and start thinking about the tree.

What this does not clear

Blocking

INV-3
The $500 floor drives the lot-scale weight. That weight is the one assumption in the ranking. If a real distribution of past job values by ZIP density exists anywhere in the record, it should replace the assumption.
INV-8
Suppression must hold across a fresh pull. A Michigan list is a brand-new data pull into ZIPs never touched before. Confirm the suppression store applies to a first-time import in a new territory before any send.
Consent
Basis must be recorded for this territory. Opening a new state is the moment to complete the consent-basis record rather than carry the gap forward. See docs/CONSENT-AND-SUPPRESSION-POLICY.md §1 in the workspace.
