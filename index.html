01

The gap is fleet-wide, not one bad account

Week of 23 August. Every tracked subaccount, side by side with what the platform actually created.

In plain terms

All 29 of 29 subaccounts booked more than the tracker reported. Not most. All of them.

The tracker is not broken and nobody is hiding anything — it reports the number that survives, which is a reasonable thing to bill on. But it means the company has never once seen the 230 appointments that were made and then died that week, and cannot tell a tier that books badly from a tier that books fine and loses them afterwards. Those need opposite fixes.

In plain terms

This is the answer to the question you actually asked — why some subaccounts do well and others don’t.

It is largely not a booking problem. It is a keeping problem. Top Notch Tier 3 loses 63% of what it books. Life’s Tree Doc loses 1%. They are running the same product.

And the timing says these are not homeowners changing their minds: on 19 of 29 tiers the typical cancellation happens within 12 hours of the booking being made, fleet median 6.3 hours. A real change of heart takes days. Something is creating appointments that get cleaned up almost immediately — and it is happening on nearly every tier.

03

Ethan is the best booker you have

Confirmed at fleet scale, not on one account. The AI books nearly three times as much as everything else and loses far less of it.

In plain terms

Ethan books 1,372 of the fleet’s 1,884 appointments and keeps 74% of them. Every other route books 512 and keeps 60%.

If anyone suggests the cancellations are the AI’s fault, this is the answer: turning Ethan off would raise the fleet cancellation rate, not lower it. The problem is the rule both paths follow, not the one following it best.

04

212,104 people have told us to stop

Exact counts, not sampled — every contact flagged do-not-contact, across 2.7 million.

In plain terms

Roughly one person in thirteen that Your Algorithm has ever messaged has asked not to be messaged again. On Harley’s Tier 1 it is one in seven.

Two reasons this matters beyond politeness. It is a consumable resource — every opt-out permanently shrinks a territory that was sold as a territory. And it is the compliance surface: 212,104 people is the population that must never be messaged again, and the suppression store meant to guarantee that is recorded as built but empty.

05

Three things nobody is looking at

And one bright spot worth copying

Life’s Tree Doc booked 76 appointments in August and lost one. A 1% cancellation rate against a fleet average of 30%, using the same platform, the same AI and the same script.

That is either the single most valuable thing in this report or a recording gap, and it takes one conversation with whoever runs that account to find out which. If it is real, it is the playbook.

What this does not show
  • August only — appointments created 1–29 Aug 2026, plus a week-of-23-Aug cut for the tracker comparison. Earlier months were not read.
  • Cancelled is not no-show. Policy counts a no-show as delivered. A cancellation is a different event, and most land before the appointment time. Whether they were billable is a definitional question for an owner, not something this report decides.
  • Tracker figures come from the Weekly Lead & Appointment Tracker, tab Aug 23 2026, and cover 29 of the 30 subaccounts. Mustard Seed – Alachua has no tracker row.
  • Opt-out counts are exact, queried per subaccount rather than sampled. An earlier sampled figure in the Tier 1 report understated this — the exact rate for that account is 9.44%, not 5.21%. Use these numbers.
  • Delivery and reply rates are still missing. No record holds attendance; message delivery and carrier failure remain unmeasured anywhere in the company, and are still the largest gap in the operating picture.
  • Nothing was written. Appointment, conversation and contact reads only, across all 30 subaccounts. No appointment, contact, conversation, tag or message was created, changed or deleted, and no message was sent.
