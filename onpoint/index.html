01 / The readA fast, well-written page that answer engines cannot quote

The site is not slow, broken or badly written — it is faster than most agency sites and the copy is specific. The problem is narrower and more fixable: almost none of what makes the company credible is in a form a machine can read, and the single most load-bearing content on the page is served as the number zero.

The headline finding

Five proof numbers — years in business, doors knocked, signatures gathered, validity rate, states worked — are rendered by JavaScript from a starting value of 0, and only when the block is scrolled into view. The served HTML says the company has zero years of experience and zero signatures. That is what a crawler that does not run JavaScript, or renders without scrolling, takes away.

Everything else in this report is ordinary technical work. That one is not, because it inverts the page’s own argument. A company whose pitch is “you only pay for work that verifiably happened” is publishing its verification as nothing.

The real figures exist in data-count. They are written into the page by an IntersectionObserver, which fires only when the element enters the viewport — so even a crawler that executes JavaScript but does not scroll leaves with zeros.

The fix is one line each

Put the real number in the HTML and let the script animate up to it from there, rather than up from a hard-coded 0. Nothing about the animation has to change and nobody viewing the page sees a difference.

04 / The marketA business with a two-year heartbeat

Signature gathering is not a steadily growing market. It is a market that nearly switches off in odd years and pays record rates in even ones, and 2026 is an even year.

What the cycle means commercially

The gap between 2024 and 2025 is roughly twenty-one to one. A firm that wins work in an even year and holds the crew together through the odd year is running a fundamentally different business from one that staffs up and disbands. On Point’s stated model — rapid crew mobilisation, pay-for-verified-work — is built for the peak, which is the right shape; the question a buyer will ask is what happens to the crew in 2027.

Florida, where On Point is based and says the business started, hosted the single most expensive signature drive of the 2024 cycle. That is a genuinely strong home-market story and the website does not tell it.

Where they actually stand, in the one state that publishes it

Colorado licenses petition entities and publishes the register, which makes it the only place their position can be measured from outside rather than asserted. On Point holds licence #2026189 and is circulating 7 petitions.

Seven active petitions places them mid-pack among active entities and comfortably ahead of the 33 that hold a licence and are circulating nothing. Against the national tier — Advanced Microtargeting, Fieldworks and PCI Consultants each ran six drives in 2024 and together accounted for 35% of all drives — this is a regional operator with real, verifiable throughput, not a national one. PCI alone claims 65 million signatures since 1992 against On Point’s 400,000.

The strategic gap

That Colorado licence number is the single most valuable asset the company is not using. It is a state-issued, independently checkable credential with their name on a government register — exactly the kind of third-party corroboration answer engines weight most heavily, and exactly what distinguishes them from an unverifiable competitor. It appears nowhere on the site.

07 / Order of workWhat to do, in order

Ranked by effect per hour of work, not by severity alone.

  1. Put the five stat numbers in the HTML. One edit, five values, no visible change to the page. It stops the site telling machines the company has done nothing.
  2. Rewrite the meta description so it matches the page: one door figure, one state count, under 160 characters. Currently 203 characters and contradicts the body twice.
  3. Add Organization and Service schema, including the Colorado licence number, the Fort Lauderdale address, and sameAs links to the Secretary of State register. The site has no structured data at all, and this is where the licence becomes machine-readable proof.
  4. Publish robots.txt and a sitemap. Both 404 today. Name the answer-engine crawlers explicitly while you are there.
  5. Fix the www certificate. www.onpointccc.com currently fails TLS outright — anyone typing it gets a browser security warning, not the site.
  6. Break the single page into service and state pages. One page can rank for one thing; 14 states and six services are one page’s worth of content doing a fraction of its work.
  7. Move the contact form off a personal Gmail. It posts to formsubmit.co addressed to a gmail.com account, which is published in the page source.
  8. Claim the brand. A search for the company name returns four unrelated “OnPoint Consulting” firms and not this one.

Method and limits. Everything here comes from the live site, the Colorado Secretary of State register, and Ballotpedia’s published cost analyses. Nothing was inferred from private data and no figure appears that is not sourced. Two apparent defects were checked and discarded rather than reported: the em dashes render correctly (they are valid UTF-8, and only looked wrong in a terminal), and the headline is not run together (it is ground.<br><em>Results). Search visibility was assessed from result pages, not from Search Console, so “no organic presence found” means exactly that and not “not indexed”. The 2026 cost figure is a reported California spot rate for two specific measures, not a national average; Ballotpedia publishes the 2026 average in October.
